## Refactoring candidate

Both in `ai-connector-service`.

| #  | Status | what                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|----|--------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| R1 | open   | An expense with no `incoming_message_id` (entered by hand, once the ledger lets a person do that) is ignored on `c`, `u` and `d` (design F15). Keep it instead, as a row that hangs off no message, with its own retention.                                         | A hand-entered expense is the most reliable example of how that person files spending. Today `recorded_expense.message_id` is `NOT NULL` and every row is purged with its message, so keeping one is a design change, not a fix. Nobody can create one yet                                |
| R2 | open   | `ChangeStreamConsumer` carries four concerns — lifecycle, group membership and reconnect, the claim/pending/new read order with its retry asymmetry, and the per-entry reader→port→ack mapping. Extract the last into a `ChangeStreamEntryHandler` (a unit target), and optionally the read cycle into a class of its own, leaving the consumer as lifecycle plus reconnect in `MemoryPurgeScheduler`'s shape. | The per-entry mapping is the most-branching piece and is reachable only through the real-Redis integration test; a change to the retry order or the outcome mapping today edits a 270-line class that also owns the thread. The testing conventions' `adapter/redis` mapping gains the handler as a unit target |
| R3 | open   | Order the store by the ledger's own order, and key names by id. (a) `applied_lsn` on `recorded_expense`, set on every write from `source.lsn`, every update guarded by `applied_lsn < :lsn`. (b) A `category` mirror table keyed by the ledger's category ids — `(id, user_id, parent_id, name, applied_lsn)`, groupings being the rows with no parent — upserted from every spending entry's enrichment and updated by id from every `category` `u`; `recorded_expense` keeps `category_id` only and drops `category_name`, `grouping_name` and `idx_recorded_expense_user_grouping`. (c) The ledger's enrichment gains `groupingId` beside `groupingName` (additive), which is what links the mirror row to its parent. Touches the ledger's contract, a connector migration and task 33's read model: a design task of its own | A consumer group hands entries to consumers with no key or partition, a claim replays a stalled entry after its successors, and the pending sweep applies a later entry past a still-failing one — so a younger change to one expense can be overwritten by an older one, and a category or grouping rename can land before or after the change it should follow. Row-keyed writes and id-keyed renames are fixed by (a); the grouping rename is keyed by name today (F7), so a chained rename (`Dining→Food`, `Food→Meals`) delivered out of order matches nothing and leaves every row on the middle name for good — (b) with (c) removes the name key entirely and needs no seeding, since every spending entry carries its enrichment. Bounded to one instance and no refused entry today; a wrong grouping name is a wrong example for task 33 that never self-heals |
