| R3 | done · 35, 37 | **Target architecture: the ledger publishes facts, not rows.** (a) The ledger gains an outbox table written in the same transaction as the change and deleted in it too, captured by the existing slot and routed onto the existing stream (Debezium's outbox event router; the `d` of the outbox row is dropped). Events name what happened, with the message as the aggregate: `ProposalCreated`, `ProposalRefiled`, `ProposalDiscarded`, `ProposalAccepted{proposalId, expenseId}`, `ExpenseRecorded`, `ExpenseRefiled`, `ExpenseRemoved`, `CategoryRenamed{id, name}`, each carrying its content, `categoryId`, `groupingId` and both names. (b) The connector applies each event as one idempotent upsert keyed by the event's ids; the txId pairing, the provisional `DISCARDED`, `UNKNOWN`, content-equality and the name-keyed grouping rename go. (c) An `applied_sequence` per row from the event's position, guarding every update, and a `category` mirror keyed by id updated from `CategoryRenamed` and seeded from each event's category fields, so a rename is one id-keyed write. Touches the ledger's contract and schema, the connector's migration and consumer, and task 33's read model: a design task of its own, before task 33 reads labels | Row-level capture makes the consumer re-derive intent from tables, and nearly every hard call in design 32 is a symptom of it: an acceptance recognised by `txId` and content, a discard that is only a guess until its partner arrives, `UNKNOWN` when that partner is dropped, a grouping renamed by name. On top of that a consumer group hands entries to consumers with no key or partition, a claim replays a stalled entry after its successors, and the pending sweep applies a later entry past a still-failing one — so a younger change can be overwritten by an older one and a chained grouping rename (`Dining→Food`, `Food→Meals`) delivered out of order leaves every row on the middle name for good. The store's purpose is a labelled corpus of a person's messages and what they did with each proposal, owned by the extraction service so it can be replaced wholesale; labels the ledger states outright are exact, carry no `UNKNOWN` class, and can grow ("the amount was edited before accepting") where rows never can. Bounded to one instance and no refused entry today. Task 35 built the ledger's outbox facts; task 37 rebuilt the connector to apply each as an id-keyed upsert guarded by `applied_sequence` |
